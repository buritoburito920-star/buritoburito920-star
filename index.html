▸ OVERVIEW

It doesn't make spaghetti.
It hunts it.

Most failure-catchers want you to flash new firmware or open up the printer. This one just reads the camera feed you already have, running entirely on a separate Raspberry Pi. Nothing on the printer itself is touched, and the whole thing installs or removes with one script.

01

Setup timer

A 185 second window while your bed and hotend settle, so the camera never mistakes warm-up for a problem.

02

Baseline build

The detector learns the calm, printing-as-expected frame it should be comparing every new one against.

03

Active watch

Every frame is checked pixel by pixel against the baseline, filtering out motion blur and nozzle shadow.

04

Auto-response

Once certainty and consecutive hits are met, it pauses, cancels, or just sends an alert. You decide which.

▸ SETUP & INSTALLATION

Four steps. One Pi. No soldering.

Verified on a Raspberry Pi 4. Other boards should work with a few small tweaks.

STEP 1 // PREP YOUR NETWORK

Grab your Raspberry Pi IP and your FlashForge printer IP (port 8899 is fixed). Optional: make a Discord webhook for personal alerts.

STEP 2 // GET YOUR TOOLS

Install PuTTY and FileZilla (or any SSH/SFTP client), then download the 3D-Spaghetti folder from GitHub.

STEP 3 // CONNECT TO YOUR PI

SSH in with PuTTY, or drag the folder over with FileZilla via SFTP on port 22.

STEP 4 // DEPLOY AND GO

cd /path/to/3D-Spaghetti

bash 3D-Spaghetti_Setup --check

bash 3D-Spaghetti_Setup

▸ HARDWARE CHECK

What it runs on.

MINIMUM
  • Raspberry Pi 3 (4GB RAM)
  • 720p Pi Camera Module
  • Quality power brick + cable
  • Fast Micro SD card
RECOMMENDED
  • Raspberry Pi 4 (8GB RAM)
  • 1080p Pi Camera Module
  • Quality power brick + cable
  • Fast Micro SD card

Software side: Raspberry Pi OS (Bullseye or Bookworm), Python 3.11, rpicam-vid / libcamera-apps, and Tailscale.

⚠ BEFORE YOU FLASH THAT SD CARD
  • Hardware fit: tuned for the FlashForge AD5M specifically. The AD5X may work but hasn't been tested.
  • Beta status: still under active development, so expect updates and the occasional bug.
  • Not foolproof: computer vision can misread a frame. Pause and Cancel are there, but use your judgment.
  • Safe to try: the install is fully reversible and won't damage your Pi or printer.
  • Keep it private: don't share your local IP or Tailscale link publicly. It's a direct line into your machine.

Sleep through the print. Not through the failure.
